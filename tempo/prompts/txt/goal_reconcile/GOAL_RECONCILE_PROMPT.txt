- **Activities** = concrete working spheres / personal projects — time-bounded, situated pursuits.
- **Life Goals** = personal strivings (Emmons, 1986) — recurring pursuits that organize multiple activities and persist beyond any single project.

########################################
# What makes a life goal (personal striving)?
########################################
A life goal must have THREE defining properties (Emmons, 1986):

1. **Dispositional recurrence** — it's an ongoing, characteristic pursuit, not a one-time objective. "Finish my dissertation" has an endpoint; "become a credible independent researcher" is something you're always working toward.
2. **Active behavioral manifestation** — it shows up in what {user_name} actually does, not just what they might wish or value. Every goal must be grounded in activity patterns you can point to.
3. **Abstraction above actions** — it organizes multiple concrete activities under a common pursuit. A striving that maps to only ONE activity is probably restating that activity, not a life goal.

A life goal answers: "What is {user_name} consistently pursuing across these activities?" Not a need label, not a personality trait, not a single project restated.

########################################
# How to write goal text (for revise / new / merge labels)
########################################
Goal text should start with **"{user_name} is ..."** and describe an ongoing personal striving — what the user is characteristically pursuing, drawn to, working toward, or actively avoiding. Vary the phrasing naturally after "is" to fit the specific goal.

**Three properties every goal text must convey:**
- **Ongoing** — reads as a recurring pattern, not a one-time task
- **Active** — describes something {user_name} is doing, not wishing
- **Above any single activity** — captures a pursuit that spans multiple behaviors

Good phrasing patterns (vary these — do NOT repeat the same construction):
- "{user_name} is pursuing [X]..." (active, ongoing)
- "{user_name} is working to [X]..." (active, effortful)
- "{user_name} is invested in [X]..." (ongoing commitment)
- "{user_name} is drawn to [X]..." (recurring attraction)
- "{user_name} is actively avoiding [X]..." (avoidance striving)
- "{user_name} is growing into [X]..." (developmental trajectory)
- "{user_name} is building [X]..." (ongoing construction)
- "{user_name} is guarding against [X]..." (protective avoidance)
- "{user_name} is trying not to [X]..." (avoidance, fear-driven)
- "{user_name} is keeping up with [X]..." (obligation-driven)
- "{user_name} is negotiating between [X] and [Y]..." (mixed/tension)

**Write in plain, everyday language.** The goal should feel like something a thoughtful friend would say, not an academic paper. Avoid jargon like "cultivating", "leveraging", "stewardship", "operationalizing", "multifaceted", "restorative variety".

Good examples (note: strivings span ALL life domains, not just work):

APPROACH strivings — pursuing something desirable:
- "{user_name} is working to become someone people trust at work — careful code reviews and owning deployments show a need to be seen as reliable"
- "{user_name} is invested in building a life with their partner — apartment hunting, budgeting together, and planning trips all point to making something shared and lasting"
- "{user_name} is feeding a restless curiosity through wide-ranging reading — browsing jumps across unrelated topics driven by what's interesting, not what's useful"
- "{user_name} is building a body they feel strong in — consistent gym tracking, meal planning, and researching training programs show this is a real commitment, not a New Year's resolution"

AVOIDANCE strivings — actively steering away from something feared:
- "{user_name} is actively avoiding shallow work — always goes back to primary sources instead of summaries, afraid of being someone who cuts corners"
- "{user_name} is guarding against financial precarity — checking accounts frequently, researching safe investments, and avoiding discretionary spending all point to a deep need for security"
- "{user_name} is trying not to lose touch with old friends — periodic check-ins and travel planning feel driven by fear of drifting apart more than spontaneous desire to connect"

MIXED strivings — attracted to and afraid of the same thing:
- "{user_name} is drawn to creative side projects but squeezed by deadlines — tool exploration and experiments happen in bursts between work sprints, pulled between the identity of a builder and the fear of falling behind"
- "{user_name} is growing into a technical leader but unsure they belong there — mentoring and driving architecture discussions point to an identity beyond writing code, but they still default to individual work when stressed"
- "{user_name} is pursuing independence from their parents' expectations while still wanting their approval — career choices and lifestyle decisions show someone negotiating between their own path and family harmony"

OUGHT strivings — obligation-driven patterns:
- "{user_name} is keeping up with administrative responsibilities they find draining — grading, email replies, and form submissions happen reactively and with low engagement, driven by obligation not interest"
- "{user_name} is staying on top of their health out of anxiety more than enthusiasm — doctor visits, symptom Googling, and insurance paperwork feel like threat management, not self-care"

Bad examples (avoid):
- "wants to be productive" — unfalsifiable, not grounded in behavior
- "{user_name} is finishing their dissertation" → project, not striving. The striving might be "pursuing recognition as a credible independent researcher"
- "is seeking competence" — that's a need tag, not a striving. What is {user_name} actually DOING?
- "is cultivating relational nurturance" — academic jargon. Try "is invested deeply in showing up for the people around them"

# Goal Orientation Dimensions
########################################
For each goal (especially revise/new/merge), also maintain:

**orientation**: `approach` (toward something desirable), `avoidance` (away from something feared), or `mixed` (simultaneously attracts and repels). Do NOT default to approach — actively look for ambivalence.

**aspiration_vs_obligation**: `ideal` (aspirational, self-chosen), `ought` (obligation, external expectation), or `mixed`.

**autonomy_dim**: `autonomous` (freely chosen), `controlled` (external pressure, guilt, reward), or `mixed`.

**identity_link**: How this goal connects to {user_name}'s hoped-for self. Populate for approach AND mixed goals.

**feared_self**: What feared self {user_name} is trying to avoid. Populate for avoidance AND mixed goals. For mixed goals, BOTH identity_link and feared_self should be populated.

########################################
# Decision criteria
########################################
For EACH candidate, choose ONE:

**match** — Candidate IS an existing goal. Same underlying striving, same behavioral pattern.
- Signals: shared activities, same domains, same proximate purpose at the striving level.
- Action: attach candidate's activities to existing goal. Keep existing label.
- Use when the candidate and existing goal describe the SAME recurring pursuit.

**revise** — Same goal, but existing label is stale, incomplete, or missing important context.
- Signals: same pursuit, but candidate reveals new people, domains, orientation, or scope.
- Also use when an existing goal's `activities:` field no longer matches its label — activities get merged and revised over time, and the goal text should stay grounded in its current evidence.
- Action: attach activities AND update label/summary. UNION people/resources — don't abstract away specifics.

**new** — Genuinely different pursuit from everything in the repository.
- Signals: different striving, different domains, different orientation/needs.
- Action: create new goal with specific, evidence-grounded label.
- Apply the completability test: if it can be checked off, it's a project, not a striving.

**merge** — Candidate reveals that 2+ EXISTING goals were actually one underlying striving.
- Signals: candidate bridges goals tracked separately but sharing the same striving/purpose/needs.
- Action: merge existing goals into one, attach candidate's activities, unified label.
- Threshold: must genuinely be the same striving accidentally split. If they're distinct pursuits that
  co-occur, use relations instead. NEVER merge aggressively — false merges lose information.

########################################
# Constraint rules
########################################
- [locked]: MUST NOT be merged, deleted, or relabeled. If candidate overlaps, prefer match.
- [user-edited]: MUST keep exact label. You may reassign activities to/from them.
- [user-provided]: User-stated goal. Strong prior — prefer matching to these when evidence supports.
- [user-reassigned]: Respect current assignment.
- User annotations outweigh behavioral inference.
- NEVER merge two goals if either is [locked].

########################################
# Confidence scoring
########################################
Rate your confidence in each reconciliation decision:

- **Evidence quality**: Is the decision supported by shared activities, domains, and behavioral patterns?
- **Striving alignment**: For match/revise — do the candidate and existing goal describe the SAME underlying striving, not just overlapping activities?
- **Merge threshold**: For merges — is there strong evidence these are genuinely the same pursuit accidentally split?

Score: **1 (weak/uncertain)** to **10 (strong evidence for this decision)**.

Be conservative — a wrong merge loses a life goal permanently. When uncertain, prefer "match" over "merge" and "new" over forcing a match. Assign high confidence (8-10) only when the behavioral evidence clearly and directly supports the decision.

########################################
# Edge cases
########################################
- **Contradictions**: If a candidate conflicts with an existing goal's purpose, keep them separate and add a "conflicts" relation.
- **Granularity mismatch**: If one goal subsumes another, prefer the version that preserves distinct strivings without redundancy.
- **Weak evidence**: Avoid aggressive merges; if evidence is ambiguous, prefer "new" to preserve information.
- **Confidence recalibration**: After deciding match/revise/merge, recalibrate confidence based on the combined evidence — the merged result may be stronger or weaker than either original.

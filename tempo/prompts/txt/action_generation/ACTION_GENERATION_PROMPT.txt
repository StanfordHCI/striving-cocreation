## What is an ACTION?

An ACTION is a goal-directed step (the "why" at the task level). Given these user operations, what is {user_name} **currently in the process of doing**?

- Think of an ACTION as a coherent task the user would describe in one sentence (e.g., "{user_name} is drafting an email to the IRB", "{user_name} is editing Overleaf paper for Sequoia study").
- Boundaries between two actions should mark a shift in {user_name}'s **intent or task**, not just a small change in app, window, or tab.
- Avoid over-segmentation for minor tweaks in the same task (e.g., switching between Overleaf and PDF viewer for the same writing task is usually ONE action).

These actions will later be clustered into long-lived ACTIVITIES (ongoing
projects and pursuits). Your goal_hint and object_label are key signals
for that clustering — make them specific and consistent across related actions.

Always reference specific applications, documents, projects, or names when they are clearly indicated in the operations (e.g., "{user_name} is editing Overleaf document for Sequoia IRB", "{user_name} is checking Slack messages from James").

Take into account things {user_name} chooses NOT to do (e.g., ignoring some notifications or tabs), as this can help you infer what they are prioritizing in the current action.

Segments must be:
- Contiguous
- Non-overlapping
- Together cover all operations 1..N

## Scoring (1–10 for each)

- **boundary_confidence**: Is there a real shift in intent at this boundary?
  High (8-10) = clear task switch. Low (1-3) = arbitrary split within same task.

- **confidence**: Does the evidence support your action description?
  High (8-10) = explicit app/document/task references, sustained interaction.
  Low (1-3) = ambiguous, brief glance, weak support.
  Be conservative. Just because an application appears on {user_name}'s screen does not mean they
  have deeply engaged with it — they may have only glanced at it. Assign high confidence (8-10)
  only when the operations show explicit, sustained engagement with specific named content.

- **decay**: How long will this action remain a meaningful description?
  High (8-10) = sustained multi-step task (writing a paper, drafting a long email).
  Low (1-3) = brief check or glance (dismissing a notification).
  Be conservative — don't inflate unless operations show sustained engagement.

## Task

Segment the operations into contiguous ACTION units and provide ONE action per segment.

For each segment:
- Explain why those operations belong together as one action.
- Explain why boundaries (start/end) are placed where they are.
- Describe the action in a way that a human would recognize as "what they were trying to do" during that span.
- Include a brief `goal_hint` that captures the higher-level motive/intent that points to their life goal  (e.g., "wants to make progress on dissertation", "wants to prioritze synchronous communication").
- Infer a lightweight metadata profile for the action (object, domain, community, rules, COM-B, habit/cue) when clearly supported by the operations; otherwise leave fields null/empty.

### Action-level metadata fields

For each ACTION, infer:
- object_label: main object/problem this action is working on (e.g., "Sequoia IRB response", "dissertation methods section"), or null.
- outcome_type: type of outcome (one of: "produce_artifact", "communicate", "plan_organize", "learn_explore", "monitor_check", "other", or null).
- domain: life/work domain (e.g., "research", "teaching", "admin", "personal_life", "health", "travel", "other", or null).
- community: main community this action is for/with (e.g., "lab", "sequoia_partners", "teaching_team", "students", "family_friends", "university_admin", or null).
- tension_hint: brief description of any apparent conflict/tension between user's goals and their tools, tasks, or actions, or null.
- engagement_state: characterizes the user's attention pattern during this action.
  One of: "sustained_focus" (deep, uninterrupted work on a single task), "fragmented" (frequent app/tab switches, multitasking), "idle" (minimal interaction, away or passive), "rapid_switching" (fast toggling between 2-3 things), or null.
- cognitive_mode: Rasmussen's skill-rule-knowledge framework — what level of cognitive effort does this action require?
  One of: "skill_based" (routine/automatic, e.g. habitual email checking), "rule_based" (following known procedures, e.g. filling forms), "knowledge_based" (novel problem-solving, e.g. debugging unfamiliar code), or null.
- initiation: what triggered this action?
  One of: "self_initiated" (user proactively started this), "externally_triggered" (notification, message, calendar event prompted it), or null.
- social_mode: is the user working alone or with others?
  One of: "solo" (individual work), "synchronous_communication" (real-time interaction — call, chat, meeting), "async_communication" (email, PR review, forum post), "passive_consumption" (reading, watching, browsing), or null.

Be conservative: only fill these fields when the evidence from operations clearly supports them; otherwise leave them null or empty.
